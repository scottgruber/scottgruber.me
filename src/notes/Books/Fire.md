### Highlights

- Humans have exhumed fossil biomass and are burning it on such an immense scale that combustion and fire regimes now extend across geologic time. What failed to burn in the old Earth is burning in the modern. The limi on fire have increasingly become only those imposed by human will. (Page 14)
- The biosphere needed a reliable spark whose timing obeyed biotic rhythms, subject to ecological processes, shaped by natural selection. Ideally ignition could be coded by instinct. A creature would set fires much as elms shed leaves or salmon turn upstream to spawn. What nature got instead was a sentient being whose neural net was shortcircuited by synapses of society and culture.
  The Earth's keeper of the flame kept it for his own purposes. By cooking food we got small guts and big heads. By cooking landscapes we went to the top of the food chain. By cooking the planet we have become a geologic force. (Page 26)
